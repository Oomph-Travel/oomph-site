# Blurb examples

All from the August 2026 batch, after Eric approved the voice. Use them to calibrate register,
not as templates to fill in — a blurb that reads as a swapped-noun version of another is doing
less work than it should.

## The default: advisory judgment

Most sailings, on ships Eric hasn't been aboard. The first-person warrant comes from *how he
chooses*, which is true and unfakeable.

**Silver Shadow · Montreal → Miami · 14 nights · Peggy's Cove, Halifax**

> Montreal in late October, when the St. Lawrence has turned, down to Miami two weeks later —
> one ship, no flights in between, and the weather improving every day you're aboard. Silver
> Shadow is small enough that the crew knows your name by the second morning. The Halifax
> morning is why this one's on my list: a private event at Peggy's Cove for my guests only,
> and it runs once.

Three moves: the season and the routing logic, one true thing about the ship, then the shore
event as the reason to choose *this* departure.

**Silver Dawn · Venice → Monte Carlo · 12 nights · Kotor**

> Twelve nights from Venice around to Monte Carlo, taking the Adriatic before winter closes it
> down. Kotor on November 8 is the day I'd protect: the old town, the cable car up the
> mountain, and tastings at Njegusi village — four and a half hours, reserved for my guests.
> Silver Dawn includes the drinks and the gratuities, so the fare is closer to the real number
> than it first looks.

"The day I'd protect" is advisory judgment. So are "why this one's on my list", "the itinerary
I'd point you to", "the one I'd hold", "my favourite stretch".

## With genuine first-hand experience

Only for ships in [ships-sailed.md](ships-sailed.md).

**EXPLORA I · Barcelona round-trip · 10 nights · Valencia**

> I've sailed EXPLORA I, and what stayed with me is how little the ship pushes you — no fixed
> dining times, no rush to be anywhere. Ten nights round-trip from Barcelona in the first week
> of November, when the western Mediterranean is still warm and largely emptied out. Valencia
> on the 6th is a private five hours ashore for my guests.

The impression here was *inferred from the product*, not supplied by Eric. That's acceptable
only because it was flagged to him for review before publishing. If he offers a real memory,
use his words instead — they'll be better than anything reconstructed.

## Same ship, two departures

Vary the angle rather than the adjectives, and let the second acknowledge the first.

**Celebrity Edge · Sydney → Auckland**

> I've sailed Celebrity Edge, and she's the ship I point people to when they want something
> large that doesn't feel large — the Infinite Veranda cabins are worth the category. This run
> leaves Sydney in November and finishes in Auckland ten nights later, which is early summer at
> both ends. The Christchurch morning ashore at Lyttelton is reserved for my guests.

**Celebrity Edge · Auckland → Sydney**

> The reverse of the November run — Auckland down to Sydney over ten nights, arriving as the
> Australian summer opens. I've sailed Celebrity Edge; the Infinite Veranda cabins are the ones
> I book. Same private Christchurch morning at Lyttelton on November 26, reserved for my guests.

## No shore event

**Silver Wind · Puerto Williams → King George Island · 9 nights · Antarctica**

> Nine nights to the Antarctic Peninsula on an ice-strengthened ship with a full expedition
> team aboard, on an unusual routing that ends at King George Island rather than retracing the
> whole way south. Early November is the front of the season — the ice still clean, the traffic
> still light. Antarctic space goes earlier than anything else I book; this is one to ask about
> now rather than in spring.

No event is promised, and the urgency is a real booking fact rather than manufactured scarcity
(which `docs/cro-rules.md` R53 rules out).

Note also what this blurb *doesn't* claim: the Drake Passage arrangement was unclear from the
page, so it's described as "an unusual routing" rather than guessing at a fly-over. When a
detail can't be confirmed, write around it and tell Eric it's open.

## Recurring failures to check for

**Third person creeping in.** "This is the itinerary that solves it" has no first-person
warrant. "This is the itinerary I'd point you to" does. The validator catches these.

**Interchangeable openings.** If the first line would fit four other sailings, it isn't earning
its place. Name the season, the routing, or the reason the date matters.

**Reused ship facts across a batch.** Two Nova-class ships in one month shouldn't both be
introduced by their asymmetric design. Give the second a different true angle.

**Unverifiable specifics.** Service dates, guest counts, and deck details are easy to get
subtly wrong and land on a live page. If it isn't in the data or genuinely well known, leave it
out — the ship's own CPT entry carries specs anyway.
