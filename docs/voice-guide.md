# Voice Guide — Oomph Travel

Tone, voice, and the words we never use. If a draft contains anything on the No List, rewrite. The list is not a guideline — it is a rule the brand keeps in order to sound like itself.

---

## Voice in one sentence

**A trusted advisor who has personally been to the place, writing to one client at a time.**

Not a salesperson. Not a travel magazine. Not a "we" — Eric is the brand, and that's the strength.

---

## Core voice principles

1. **Use "I" not "we."** Oomph is a solo advisor. "I take on six custom Italy itineraries a quarter" is the voice; "Our team curates bespoke journeys" is not.
2. **Specifics beat adjectives.** "Sailed 14 Silversea voyages" beats "experienced cruise expert." "Eleven days between Lecce and Polignano a Mare" beats "an Italian adventure."
3. **Name the place, the season, the milestone.** "Puglia, in spring" beats "an Italian getaway." "Their 50th anniversary" beats "a special occasion."
4. **Frame for gain and legacy, not loss.** "Make this 50th anniversary the one they'll talk about for the next 30 years" — not "Don't miss this once-in-a-lifetime opportunity."
5. **Sensory language is allowed and encouraged** when it earns its place: sun-warmed, mist-veiled, candlelit, sea-salted, hand-poured, family-run, third-generation, vine-draped, marble-cooled.
6. **No superlatives.** Drop "the best," "ultimate," "amazing," "incredible."
7. **First-hand experience markers** in every blog post. "When I sailed the Silver Nova in March 2025." "On my fourth visit to Locorotondo." "The first time I stayed at the Borgo Egnazia."
8. **One sentence per social post.** If a second sentence is needed, write a journal post and link to it.

## CTA copy — the only voice that earns the click

### Never use as a primary CTA

"Submit," "Learn More," "Click Here," "Get Started," "Contact Us," "Book Now," "Get a Quote."

### Use first-person CTAs

| Generic | Oomph |
|---|---|
| Submit | Book My Discovery Call |
| Learn More | See How I Work |
| Get Started | Start Planning My Trip |
| Download | Send Me the Cabin Guide |
| Sign Up | Get Italy Trip Tips |
| Book a Call | Book a Discovery Call → |

The Discovery Call CTA is always **"Book a Discovery Call →"** — that exact phrasing, with the arrow. Never "Schedule a Consultation," never "Book Now."

Microcopy directly under it:
> Free 30-minute call. No pressure, no obligation.

## Section eyebrows, page chrome, micro-content

Eyebrows are how we cite the source. They're not decoration:

- "Field Note · 04"
- "Silversea · Cabin Note"
- "From the Journal"
- "One Advisor"
- "Press · Puglia"

Itinerary cover anatomy: **Volume number · Place + season · Who prepared it.**

- "Itinerary · March 2026 · Vol. III"
- "Puglia, in spring."
- "Eleven days between Lecce and Polignano a Mare, with a quiet stop in Locorotondo."
- "— prepared by Megan Carlton, Travel Advisor, Oomph Travel LLC"

Never "your trip" or "your getaway." Always the place and the season.

## Tagline

**Life is short — travel with Oomph.**

That exact em-dash, that exact casing. Reserve for closing moments — the business card back, the email footer, the social bio. Don't open with it. Don't repeat it three times on a page.

---

## Photography in copy context

The photo description is part of the voice. The caption should never describe the photo literally ("a couple at sunset"). It should name the place, the season, and what's happening:

- "Locorotondo, late afternoon. The Wednesday market is still putting itself away."
- "Silver Nova, Veranda Deck, dawn watch. The Strait of Gibraltar is two hours astern."

Never: "Champagne flutes · balcony · sunset." That photo isn't selling Oomph; it's selling a stock image of the luxury cruise cliché.

---

## When in doubt

Read the draft aloud. If it sounds like a brochure, rewrite. If it sounds like Eric on a call with a client he genuinely likes, ship it.
